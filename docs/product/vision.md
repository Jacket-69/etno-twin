# Vision — etno-twin

## The problem

Reconstructing individual orbits is not the open problem. Rubin's solar system
processing pipeline links detections across nights, fits preliminary orbits, reports
discoveries to the Minor Planet Center and ingests the resulting orbital catalogue
back into its own products. Orbits arrive already solved.

The open problem sits one layer above, and has two components that today are handled
separately:

- **Short-arc orbital uncertainty.** For large-orbit objects — precisely the ones of
  dynamical interest — a single opposition of observations leaves uncertainties above
  100 au in semi-major axis. This is not a limitation of the fitting algorithms: the
  problem is intrinsically degenerate and only additional astrometry over years
  resolves it.
- **Observational selection bias.** Each survey observes a portion of the sky, to a
  depth and with follow-up criteria of its own, so an observed catalogue is never a
  representative sample of the real population. Correcting this with survey simulators
  is established practice — but it is applied treating each orbit as a known point
  value.

The question this project addresses: given detections of ETNOs from heterogeneous
surveys, each with its own selection function, and with individual orbits subject to
large uncertainties, what can legitimately be claimed about the orbital structure of
the underlying population when both sources of uncertainty are propagated jointly —
and how much statistical power is gained over current methods?

## Who it is for

- **Primary user — the domain client.** An astrophysicist who needs a platform that
  turns population hypotheses into testable predictions against real survey data.
- **Secondary — the field.** The reference implementation for survey-bias correction
  is Fortran 77/95 packaged in 2018, with no automated tests and no modern packaging.
  There is concrete room for contemporary engineering.
- **Reviewers of the thesis**, who evaluate the system, not the astronomy.

## What it delivers

1. **Validated method** on synthetic populations with known ground truth, generated by
   applying real selection functions.
2. **Verdict on the real catalogue**, with orbital uncertainty propagated inside the
   forward model.
3. **Statistical power curve** against the classical method implemented in-project as
   a baseline: how much is gained by combining heterogeneous surveys, and how many
   additional Rubin detections would be needed to discriminate the competing
   hypotheses.

The primary metric of the contribution is statistical power against the baseline —
**not a discovery**. With a sample of a few dozen objects, "inconclusive" is an
expected outcome and remains publishable.

## Success criteria

Two planes, weighted equally:

**System.** Campaign throughput in object-visits per unit time and scaling with cores
and nodes · cost of reproducing a complete experiment, in compute time and in manual
steps · reproducibility, understood as a third party regenerating every result from
this repository with one command · test coverage over the critical data-transformation
and external-simulator-integration components.

**Inference.** Statistical power against the in-project baseline over the same samples
· posterior calibration, including credible-interval coverage · measured effect of
orbital uncertainty on conclusions, contrasting object-as-distribution against
object-as-point.

Quantitative thresholds are fixed after the baseline is reproduced, when there is a
measurement of the starting point. Client acceptance criteria are agreed separately
and are subordinate to the academic evaluation criteria.

## Out of scope

- Discovering new objects or detecting on images. The project consumes catalogues, it
  does not produce them.
- Improving individual orbit determination — an intrinsic degeneracy that only more
  astrometry resolves.
- Interstellar objects. The platform is left capable of addressing them; the expected
  detection rate is too low to support validation within the project window.
- Operating a production service with live compute.
